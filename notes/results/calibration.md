# Calibration of the signal model, and graded rewards

Generated by `python -m analysis.report` from the stored logs.

The 12 runs of seeds 1, 2 and 3 in `trial/`, `cost-full/` and `seed3/`, pooled over the seeds. The pilot is left out: its log does not record the level of each group. A group is the G = 8 answers to one task. It has signal when the population standard deviation of its rewards is at least 1e-6 (`samplers.has_signal`), in practice when they are not all equal: only such a group changes the model. An answer succeeds when its reward is at or above the `success_threshold` of its run's sampler settings; uniform logs no sampler settings, and is scored with the curriculum sampler's default, 1.0. Thresholds in these runs: 1.0. The first quarter of a run is the first quarter of its steps, rounded up, and the last quarter its last quarter, rounded down.

## Predicted and observed groups with signal

If the 8 answers of a group were independent, each a success with chance p, the group would have signal with chance 1 - p^8 - (1 - p)^8. The curriculum methods build their sampling weights from this chance. For them p is the sampler's own estimate for the level (`p_hat`) when it drew the step: a step samples, generates and then reports its groups, and the log records the estimate after the reports, so a step is drawn with the estimate logged at the previous step, and the first step with 0.5, the estimate of a level not seen yet. Uniform has no estimate: its p is the share of the level's answers that succeeded, in the same run and window. Predicted is the mean of that chance over the groups, observed the share of the groups that had signal.

## Correlation of successes inside a group

The last quarter of each run. p is the share of answers that succeeded and k the successes in a group. rho = (Var(k) / (8 p (1 - p)) - 1) / 7, with Var the population variance over the groups, estimates the correlation between two answers to the same task: 0 if they were independent, as the binomial model assumes, and 1 if every group either all succeeded or all failed; it also absorbs any change in skill within the window, and any difference between the seeds, since both make some groups easier than others. It is a dash when p is exactly 0 or 1, or below 10 groups; p is rounded, so a p written as 1.000 can still have a rho. The last two columns are the share of groups in which all 8 answers failed, observed and as the binomial model gives it, (1 - p)^8.

## How graded the rewards are

Every training answer of the runs. A reward strictly between 0 and 1 is partial credit. A group can have signal without a success when its rewards differ below the threshold; the binomial model counts only successes, so it does not see such a group. The last column counts groups with successes and failures but no signal; it is 0 unless the rewards of such a group are so close that their population standard deviation is below 1e-6.
